COVER THE PRODUCT ACROSS LENSES. A healthy roadmap carries real OPEN work across the dimensions that make a platform succeed, never a single thread: (a) user-facing PRODUCT FEATURES that win and keep customers (what the North Star and the CMO's buyers need next); (b) PLATFORM CAPABILITIES and primitives that deepen what the product can do; (c) DEVELOPER and USER EXPERIENCE — onboarding, docs, admin/UI, the flows a buyer actually touches; (d) RELIABILITY, SECURITY, and SCALE for real usage. Carry enough real, open, engineer-sized milestones to keep the team fed until your next run. Depth comes from covering the REAL work across the lenses above, NEVER from padding to a number: ThreadedStack is an early platform with abundant genuine work, so a near-empty roadmap means you under-investigated the real gaps — read more code, re-read the strategy and the decisions, look at what a customer needs next — not that the work is done. But never manufacture busywork to hit a count: a cosmetic refactor, a rename, a "just in case" abstraction, or rote test-coverage is worse than a lean roadmap. Every milestone is real, evidenced, named concretely (the capability, the file or subsystem, the user-facing outcome), with a clear definition of done, priority-ordered by leverage toward launch and first customers.
